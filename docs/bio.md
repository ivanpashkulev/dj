# Summary
My name is Ivan Pashkulev, a Bulgarian DJ, turntablist and a true party rocker. 

# Background
## Childhood
I grew up in the village of Garmen in a musical family with lots of songs, dances and fun. My parents are musicians and they were playing and singing almost every evening at home with lots of friends. Our house was a party house where you can never be bored. My parents were the heart and soul of the company, bringing smiles and soul food for everyone around. Their repertoare was enormous, as my father says: "We can play for 3 days (72 hours) and not stop, without repeating a single song". They played a variety of genres - rock, pop, Bulgarian folklore, Russian "chastushki" songs (let's not forget it was comunism back then, thus the Russian influence), ballads, kid songs,you name it. 

During the late 70's, 80's and early 90's they had a local band called "Integral" together with my uncles. I still remember that on some of their concerts, at the early age of 6 - I was their lights operator behind the stage. I had to press random buttons on a lighting console with only one strict rule - put the strong white colors at the end of each song and turn them off once the next song begins. You can imagine what a fun it was for a little kid to trigger random colorful lights on the stage at that time. On May 14th 1983 they win a national championship for new emerging bands held in the town of Blagoevgrad. Back then, my mom has been pregnant in 9th month with me and I was born on the very next day - May 15th.

I've been involved in music and, more precisely, DJing since early childhood. Even then I knew that when I grew up I wanted to become a DJ.

## Teenage years
My first attempts at mixing were in 5-6th grade on two-cassette tape recorders during the mid 90's. For the first time I touched more professional DJ equipment in 9th grade. This happended in the local disco clubs in the small resort town of Chepelare where I spent my high school years. There I had the chance to play on cassette decks and CD players with pitch controls which allowed me to start gaining experience in beat matching and creating smooth transitions between the tracks. This happened in the late 90s. Back then I played the hits that were hot on MTV and VIVA music channels, but at the same time I was strongly influenced by the rave wave. I listened to Prodigy, Chemical Brothers, Underworld, Westbam, Leftfield, Josh Wink, Faithless and many more. 

In the next few years I moved on to a harder tech-house and techno sound. I'm talking about names like Jeff Mills, Carl Cox, Umek, Marco Carola, Joey Beltram, etc. I was a crazy fan of METROPOLIS (the biggest electronic music promoter in Bulgaria at the time) and I almost never missed techno parties. Influenced by the electronic music scene back then, made me obsessed with the idea of ​​playing on turntables. I first touched turntables when I was 17. This happened in the year of 2000 in Sunny Beach resort at the legendary techno and house club ZOUK, which was unique for its time. There I met DJ Chris Davis (the resident), who noticed that I was hooked and in a few days and weeks, he introduced me to the world of vinyl. He had a lot of records and gave me the unique opportunity to play and practice with them. That's when I first realized that dreams come true. I became a kind of second resident in one of the best clubs in the country, playing among world renowned guest DJs and in one summer I started mixing at a pretty decent level on turntables. I started playing at parties in front of thousands of ravers. I was only 17 years old and the feeling was incredible! I returned as a DJ superstar to Chepelare and during my last year of high school I organized techno parties in the school sports hall. At that time I also bought my first turntable.

## University years
Over the next few years I stepped away from the DJ scene and turned to audio production. I enrolled to study Computer Music Creation at the New Bulgarian University, where I studied solfegio, piano, music theory. For about 2 years I achieved quite impressive results, so good that I was encouraged to quit New Bulgarian Univesity and move to the National Musical Academy to become a proffesional musician / composer. For better or worse I didn't manage to do it. Honestly, the level of piano playing that I needed to reach was quite high. I had to compete with other students who play piano since kinder garden. I'm talking about playing Bach, Bethoven, Mozart, Chopin... which I still play but I admit - not at the needed level. 

## Radio years
Later I landed my first job and became a sound engineer in the production studio of radio FM+. And that's when I left my university education. It turned out that in about 2-3 months I learned much more in Radio FM+ than in 3 years at NBU. And at a certain point it became pointless for me to waste my parents' money for university. For a few years I gained serious experience in audio production and the results were many awards, year after year at the media festival in Albena (black sea resort). Cubase, Adobe Audition, Waves plugins became my second nature. Later I went through the Bulgarian National Radio for 2 years but to be honest the work there was quite boring and I actually used my time to study Software Engineering during this period since the incomes from radio were not paying for paragliders, vacations and led to very limited life oppurtunities.

# Turntablism
And now let's get back to the topic of DJing. One day in 2006, a colleague of mine from FM+ asked me if I've watched "SCRATCH - The Movie". I had not watched it and that same evening after work I downloaded it. Watching this movie back then was a life changing event and a turning point in my story. After this movie I knew what I wanted to do, how I wanted to do it and very soon after that I took a bank loan and bought a top battle mixer (Vestax PMC08), two Technics 1200 turntables, 10 battle records and a bunch of DVDs by Qbert, Shortee and Roc Raida to develop as a turntablist. I was like a kid on a chocolate diet. Started spending hours and hours every day to develop my techical skills - from baby srcatches, through chirps and transformers, to one and two click flare orbits, crabs and some of the most fascinating techniques for the crowd like beat juggling and body tricks. I practiced every single scrach with both hands which gave me huge leverage over the competition. I watched all DMC finals since 1986 and became quite aware of who is who in DJing. I had my favourites, starting with DJ QBert who thought me so much with his DIY 1 and DIY 2 DVDs. Later a bought also "Scharchlopedia Beaktanica - Over 100 secret scratches" DVD. Other DJs that I admired were Mix Master Mike, DJ Shadow, DJ Flare, DJ Craze, Roc Raida and all the rest from the The X-Ecutioners DJ crew, Z-Trip and many others. The list is long but I'm pretty sure you get the idea. 

Inspired by DJ Babu from The Beat Junkies who was the first DJ to describe himself as a turntablist - a musician who performs on a turntable, I decided to call myself "Vankata - The Turntablist". This is the artistic name that most people recognize me until today. Turntablism and Turntablist were kind of "Alien" terms back then in Bulgaria. Nobody knew what is that. It was even difficult to pronounce or write, so I was constatly asked what the heck is that, how to be written, etc. And that was part of my goal - to spread the knowledge and educate the Bulgarian DJ scene about the Art of Turntablism.

# Battle DJing
In 2009 I entered my first DJ competition and not just any competition but the Bulgarian National DMC - probably the most technical DJ competition ever. I came second, which was quite a success. It gave me confidence and I continued pushing my skills further. Later in the summer of 2013 I participated in RedBull3Style where I won one of the regional finals and let's say I was close to winning the final, but..... in the end I didn't win, I was kind of sabotaged but that doesn't matter any more. In 2014 I appeared as a team with DJ Markony at the SPS competition qualifiers where we blew them away and everyone expected us to win, but in the end we didn't appear in the final because we decided that the competition rules didn't suit us. In 2015 we again appeared as a team with DJ Markony at the "Golden DJ of Bulgaria" competition and there we won first place in the Freestyle category. This was my last DJ competition. Next, I entered the world of Software Development and changed my life path.

# Influence from the BBoy scene
A very important moment in this whole story is my meeting with my friend Nasko Marinov aka BBoy Swipy. We met with him in the town of Sopot in the summer of 2010. We had both decided to become acrobatic paragliding pilots and so the paragliding community brought us together. Over the next few years he introduced me to the bboy community and slowly made me understand what it means to be TRUE. I discovered the funky sound, all the old school classic breaks and beats, so many tracks and artists that I had never heard before. I discovered a community that understood and supported me, encouraged and believed in me and so I gradually started to feel as part of a big HIP HOP family and this continues until today.

# From 2015 untill now
For the last more than 10 years I've made lots of gigs. People recognize me as a true Party Rocker and I'm still amazed of the great feedback I receive every time from the audience and random people. My combination of diverse music styles, spiced up with lots of scratching and good vibes is always capturing people's hearts. I still play with my 2 Technics 1210mk2 turntables, only the mixer is newer. Currently using Rane 72 mixer, playing with Serato DJ. I've played all over Bulgaria and I'm still DJing although not that actively as before. Main reason for this is the transformation from an artist and musician to a tech person, constantly studying new technologies, working on different projects, following tech trends and lately on the path to become an AI Engineer. I moved to live from Sofia to Bansko in order to be closer to the mountain. During the winter ski season, which is quite active, I get more gigs in the local bars, participating in festivals and events. I have played over the years at many BBoy Battles, friends weddings, corporate events, but the truth is that at some point I started to refuse gigs outside of Bansko. The pay was not worth the travel and time spent driving. So you my safely conclude that my DJ career is in decline but this is about to change and that is why I now prepare this document so we can make a 180 turn and bring the beats back!
